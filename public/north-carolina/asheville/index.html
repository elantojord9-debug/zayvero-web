A BETTER WAY TO EXPLORE

Discover Asheville
your way.

✳ Especially good for: Downtown public art, native gardens and family science

Asheville can be explored without treating the mountains and downtown as a single hurried itinerary. Start at the city's central public square, then choose a separate nature outing near the university or an indoor science visit.

Pack Square Park is a useful downtown starting point, while Asheville Botanical Garden focuses on Southern Appalachian plants and limits certain activities to protect them. The interactive Asheville Museum of Science provides a ticketed option when a family wants indoor learning.

Pack Square Park

This downtown green space brings walking paths, public art and a popular seasonal splashpad into one central area. It's a relaxed family stop, although live programming and splashpad operation need a check before visiting.

Address: 80 Court Plaza, Asheville, NC 28801

Admission: Free general park entry

General public park access is free; check special-event fees and splashpad schedules.

City park dogs must stay on leash; follow local restrictions. Official dog policy ↗

02
Parks & outdoors
Family-friendly

Asheville Botanical Garden

Explore the region's native plants on paths designed for quiet nature study rather than sports. Families are welcome with supervision, but the operator forbids pets except service dogs.

Address: 151 W.T. Weaver Blvd., Asheville, NC 28804

Admission: Free admission and parking

Admission and parking are free. Some paths are not accessible to all visitors; no dogs or other pets except service animals.

03
Family activities
Family-friendly

Asheville Museum of Science (AMOS)

This downtown museum gives children and adults hands-on ways to explore science and the environment. General admission is paid for most visitors, with some age-based and eligibility discounts listed by the museum.

Address: 43 Patton Ave., Asheville, NC 28801

Admission: Paid admission — check current tickets

Check the official ticket page for current prices, available exhibits and operating hours. No dedicated on-site parking.

GOOD TO KNOW

Questions about Asheville

Are all three Asheville places free?+

No. Public parks and some cultural places are free, while museum admission or optional activities cost money. Each listing identifies which fees apply.

Can I bring a dog to every attraction?+

No. We mark Pet-friendly only where verified official rules allow dogs. Indoor museums and special gardens may ban pets; always check the operator’s rules.
