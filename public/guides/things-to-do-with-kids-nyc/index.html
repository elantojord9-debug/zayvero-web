TRAVEL WITH KIDS · NEW YORK

Things to Do With Kids in NYC: Parks, Ferry & Museums

New York is full of family experiences that don't require taking children to one expensive attraction after another. Parks and a free harbor ferry can be the center of an excellent day, while museums are useful when your group wants science or an indoor alternative. The city's size makes neighborhood planning essential—an itinerary that looks short in a list may cross several boroughs.

Family planning note

Check each operator's official website for age suitability, opening hours, safety rules, admissions and reservations. Our descriptions do not imply that every location is free or that we have personally visited it.

PLACES TO EXPLORE

What makes sense for your family?

01 · Outdoor play and city scenery

Central Park playgrounds

Free public park; optional attractions paid

Central Park has many child-friendly areas, paths and playgrounds. Select a specific area based on the ages of your children rather than trying to traverse the full park. Zoo admission, rentals and nearby museums have separate rules.

02 · Harbor experience for families

Staten Island Ferry

Free for passengers

The city-operated Staten Island Ferry is a free harbor crossing. It is not an admission ticket to the Statue of Liberty, and you should not pay anyone selling ferry passes. Check service information and bring layers for wind on outdoor decks.

03 · Waterfront park and performance space

Little Island

Free park admission; some events paid

Little Island is an elevated Hudson River park with planted paths, views and areas to explore. Public entry is free, while certain performances can have ticket requirements. Check weather closures and park access before visiting.

Official visitor information ↗Find on Maps ↗
04 · Dinosaurs, science and hands-on learning

American Museum of Natural History

Admission varies by residence and exhibition

The Museum of Natural History is a strong indoor educational outing. General admission and extra exhibitions have different terms, and discounts or pay-what-you-wish rules may depend on residency. Review the official tickets and current exhibits before promising your family an inexpensive day.

Official visitor information ↗Find on Maps ↗
05 · Waterfront playgrounds and skyline views

Brooklyn Bridge Park

Public park; optional attractions separate

Brooklyn Bridge Park provides pathways, park areas and varied play spaces, with views of the Manhattan skyline. It is a different neighborhood and outing from Central Park. Check where the playgrounds are located and plan transit or walking accordingly.

A BETTER FAMILY DAY

Three steps before you leave.

  1. Keep the day in one borough or neighborhood cluster whenever possible, especially with strollers.
  2. For a free-focused family day, use one park or ferry journey as the anchor and account for transit costs.
  3. For a museum day, verify admissions, age-appropriate exhibitions and ticketed extras directly with the operator.
