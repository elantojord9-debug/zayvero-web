your way.

✳ Especially good for: Waterside walks, hands-on exhibits and a landmark library

Indianapolis combines a downtown public park with cultural sites that work on quite different schedules. A White River State Park stroll offers a low-cost starting point before choosing which ticketed activities merit more time.

The Children's Museum of Indianapolis is an interactive family attraction north of downtown; Central Library offers a free indoor alternative. Plan these as separate stops and check live driving or transit distances rather than assuming all three are adjacent.

White River State Park

The canal and surrounding downtown green spaces offer an easy walking outing without buying a museum ticket. Families can explore the paths, while neighboring attractions and boat rentals are separate paid choices.

Address: 801 W Washington St., Indianapolis, IN 46204

Admission: Free public park entry

Free park entry, but parking, rentals and attractions may charge. Park rules permit leashed dogs on leashes no longer than six feet.

Dogs on leashes up to six feet are allowed under park rules. Official dog policy ↗

The Children's Museum of Indianapolis

Hands-on exhibits connect science, history and creativity for families and younger visitors. Prices vary depending on your visit date, so select tickets on the official site before planning a full museum day.

Address: 3000 N Meridian St., Indianapolis, IN 46208

Admission: Paid admission — prices vary by date

Advance reservations are recommended; verify date-specific rates and closures. Most visits require paid admission.

Indianapolis Central Library

The Central Library brings a historic limestone structure together with a modern glass-and-steel addition. It offers a quiet indoor setting for books and architecture without a general admission ticket.

Address: 40 E St. Clair St., Indianapolis, IN 46204

Admission: Free public library entry

Entry to the public library is free. Check the official branch page for opening hours, events and individual room restrictions.

GOOD TO KNOW

Questions about Indianapolis

Are all three Indianapolis places free?+

No. Public parks and some cultural places are free, while museum admission or optional activities cost money. Each listing identifies which fees apply.

Can I bring a dog to every attraction?+

No. We mark Pet-friendly only where verified official rules allow dogs. Indoor museums and special gardens may ban pets; always check the operator’s rules.
