Discover Portland
your way.

✳ Especially good for: Free rose gardens, interactive science and a city-block bookstore

Portland's attractions reward planning by neighborhood rather than packing every destination into one walking route. The International Rose Test Garden occupies a hillside garden setting, while OMSI is across the river in a separate district.

Powell's City of Books adds an independent indoor stop where browsing is free but shopping is optional. Consider separating the rose garden, museum and bookstore into different parts of a day, and check transit or map directions for current travel time.

International Rose Test Garden

A large rose garden in Washington Park gives visitors free outdoor paths, planting displays and views of the city. Parking can cost extra and the City of Portland explicitly requires dogs to stay on leash.

Address: 400 SW Kingston Ave., Portland, OR 97210

Admission: Free garden entry

Free public garden admission. Parking can cost extra and access may change for events or maintenance.

The City of Portland states all dogs must be leashed in this park. Official dog policy ↗

02

Family-friendly

Oregon Museum of Science and Industry (OMSI)

OMSI's interactive science spaces are designed for hands-on discovery across several age groups. General entry is paid, and selected exhibitions, presentations or experiences can require additional tickets.

Address: 1945 SE Water Ave., Portland, OR 97214

Admission: Paid museum admission — verify rates

Check the museum's current admission calendar, closures and optional ticket upgrades on its official website.

Official visitor information ↗See on Google Maps ↗
03
Arts & culture
Family-friendly

Powell's City of Books

The famous independent bookstore provides a city-block collection of books to browse at your own pace. Entry does not require a ticket, but shopping and optional purchases are separate costs.

Address: 1005 W Burnside St., Portland, OR 97209

Admission: Free to browse; purchases cost extra

Browsing has no admission fee. Check current shop hours and any special-event rules on the retailer's official site.

GOOD TO KNOW

Questions about Portland

Are all three Portland places free?+

No. Public parks and some cultural places are free, while museum admission or optional activities cost money. Each listing identifies which fees apply.

Can I bring a dog to every attraction?+

No. We mark Pet-friendly only where verified official rules allow dogs. Indoor museums and special gardens may ban pets; always check the operator’s rules.
